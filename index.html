01 / THE ONE-MINUTE VERSION

Trust is local.
Consequences aren't.

Human communities store value through trust, remembered contributions and informal reciprocity. But those memories are fragile, difficult to verify, and usually impossible to carry across social boundaries.

The goal is not a global score deciding who's good. It is portable, contestable evidence that helps people decide whom to trust — and on what terms.

Without that evidence, every new relationship bears a discovery cost. Some people earn trust over years and have little to show for it elsewhere. Others can exploit informational gaps, favorable local connections or selective histories.

We need an alternative to blind trust without handing the world a centralized mechanism for blacklisting people. Proof of Agent offers a small, unusually concrete laboratory.

02 / THE PROBLEM

Obscurity is a form of power.

Where conduct is hard to verify, trust depends on proximity, status and whoever controls the story.

01

Honest contribution disappears

Reliable work may never become visible outside the immediate relationship.

02

Bad conduct resets

Relevant histories may be inaccessible when actors enter a new setting.

03

Connections override evidence

Powerful endorsements can outweigh a weak or disputed broader record.

04

Everyone pays a trust tax

Uncertainty raises the cost of cooperation even for honest participants.

We already have informal social equity: people remember help, integrate information, and return value later. Our conversation with Max illustrated this. Its existence is not the problem. Its fragility, limited portability and weak inspectability are.

Transparency can reduce deception's informational advantage — but only if access to evidence does not create new surveillance, coercion or unjust exclusion.

03 / THE PROTOCOL PRECEDENT

What Proof of Agent actually does

Proof of Agent (PoA) is an experimental AI-agent network where verifiable actions affect eligibility, rewards and standing. It is not a general measure of character.

What is protocol-grounded

Agents answer challenges, participate in transaction attestation and serve on grading panels. Answer settlement, rewards, slashes, ejections and Established standing are recorded in public protocol data. Standing is tied to eligible, passing work and can lapse without continued qualifying participation. POAScan methodology explains how the public records are interpreted.

What must not be conflated

PoA's Established status is a defined network standing, not a blanket verdict on personal ethics. POAScan's composite Score (0–100) is an independent explorer metric assembled from standing, grading, forum activity and consistency — explicitly not an official PoA grade or rule. Its weighting reflects design choices.

Protocol nuance. POAScan's current documentation says eligible passing answers establish and maintain standing; merely serving grading seats does not grant or preserve Established status. Some network data and explorer results may lag. Hosted account state is authoritative for that account.

What transfers to human communities isn't the token or a single number. It's the pattern of identifiable action → evidence → independent evaluation → limited consequence → review.

04 / THE PROPOSAL

Proof of Reciprocity

A decentralized evidence layer for relationships, contributions, commitments and accountability — with no global moral ranking.

Three dimensions, not one score

Contribution: What did the participant demonstrably do? Conduct: How reliably did they fulfill obligations in a particular context? Reciprocity: Did the interaction produce acknowledged mutual value, and under what terms?

These dimensions can disagree. Excellent technical output does not prove fair dealing. Popularity does not prove reliability. A poor outcome does not automatically prove misconduct.

Participation should be informed, not mechanically forbidden

Reputation can shape collateral, privileges, permissions, due diligence, or expectations in voluntary settings. A newcomer might receive limited but real opportunities. A mixed history might warrant safeguards rather than exclusion. Public goods and essential services should not be gated by an unappealable private score.

Make the evidence portable. Keep interpretation plural. Leave agency with the participants.
05 / FAILURE MODES AND GUARDRAILS

How not to build a social-credit machine

Every useful reputation system can be weaponized. The design must assume adversaries include powerful institutions, not only individual fraudsters.

01 · False accusations and coordinated retaliation

Require source authentication, dispute status, independent corroboration and visible corrections. Never equate allegation counts with guilt or verified misconduct.

02 · Surveillance and permanent punishment

Publish minimal proofs rather than indiscriminate personal histories. Support selective disclosure, retention limits, redress, expiration and rehabilitation.

03 · Sybil identities and reputation laundering

Use context-appropriate identity proof, graph analysis and cost-of-forgery controls, while preserving legitimate pseudonymity. Do not equate real-name enforcement with honesty.

04 · Reputation cartels and nepotistic capture

Let multiple independent issuers attest claims. Show issuer reliability and conflicts of interest. Make local endorsements inspectable without allowing one network to monopolize legitimacy.

05 · Popularity mistaken for trustworthiness

Separate social engagement, skill, financial solvency, integrity and domain reliability. Avoid one opaque composite score; show the evidence, its uncertainty and the chosen aggregation method.

06 · Misuse by employers, states and powerful actors

Design for privacy and proportionality, protect basic rights, and test legal constraints before deploying human reputation gates. No automatic career penalties or irreversible access restrictions based solely on algorithmic outputs.

Deliberate constraint: this is a discussion about voluntary, contestable trust infrastructure, not a proposal for compulsory global scoring of individuals. Reputation can help people exercise informed choice only if those being evaluated can contest, contextualize and sometimes withhold sensitive information.
06 / A BOUNDED FIRST EXPERIMENT

Start small. Prove the retrieval.

Before designing global reputation, test whether a tiny group can maintain an accurate, useful and fair record of voluntary contributions.

I.

One opt-in community

Volunteers document contributions to a bounded shared project; no employment, housing or public-service consequences.

II.

Signed evidence

Participants attest deliverables, commitments and outcomes, with timestamps and source references.

III.

Retrieval with dissent

A lookup returns evidence and disagreements, not one unchallengeable verdict.

IV.

Adversarial evaluation

Test sockpuppets, reciprocal endorsement rings, false claims, corrections, and recovery from mistakes.

Success would mean lower verification effort, accurate attribution, useful dispute handling and no major unfair exclusion. Failure should be measurable and published. The first deliverable is a demonstrably better informed decision process — not a token, a marketplace or a universal ranking.

07 / OPEN RESEARCH QUESTIONS

What would change our minds?

What constitutes a verifiable contribution?

Can work quality be evidenced without equating it to hours, popularity, compensation or output volume?

How does reciprocity differ from reputation?

Reputation describes evidence about an actor; reciprocity concerns a relationship and its distribution of benefits. A protocol may need both perspectives.

How do good-faith newcomers gain access?

A cold-start participant needs low-risk opportunities without requiring a preexisting elite to approve them.

Can contextual reputation really be portable?

A trusted maintainer in one community may lack relevant evidence for a completely different role. Portability must not erase context.

Who can challenge the assessors?

What happens when an attester is biased, compromised, or acting for a coalition? How do we preserve dissent?

What must never enter the ledger?

Personal secrets, intimate details, unnecessary identifiers and sensitive accusations should not become immutable public artifacts.
